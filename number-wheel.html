The number wheel

A computer counts like a car’s odometer with only two digits per wheel. And a fixed number of wheels means the numbers do not run along a line: they go round in a circle. Everything strange about computer arithmetic follows from that circle.

3 · On the bench: eight switches, eight lights

This is the first thing to build, and every later test uses it. Eight DIP switches, each with a 10 kΩ resistor pulling its line down to 0 V so an open switch reads a clean 0; each line drives an LED through a 330 Ω resistor to ground. Click the switches.

The lights never say “−5”. They show a pattern. Whether 11111011 means 251 or −5 is decided by whoever reads it, which is you now, and later a circuit you build.
